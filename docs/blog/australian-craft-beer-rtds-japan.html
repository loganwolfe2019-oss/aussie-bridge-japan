Five Reasons Australian Craft Beer and RTDs Are Built for the Japanese Market

Japanese Alcohol Market18 June 2026Founder, Aussie Bridge Japan3 min read

People ask me why we bet the whole business on one corridor — Australia to Japan. Here's the short version.

1. Japan buys places, not just products

Japanese consumers care deeply about where things come from. It's the same instinct that celebrates regional sake and Kobe beef. A beer from the Sunshine Coast or a ginger beer from the high country isn't just a drink over there — it's a postcard. Australian craft carries that story without even trying.

2. The palate fit is real

Two decades of craft culture have taught Japanese drinkers to love hop-forward, fruit-driven, complex flavours — exactly where Australian brewing lives. And alcoholic ginger beer? Japan barely makes it. That's genuine white space, and white space never stays white for long.

3. Imported craft is a status symbol

In a Japanese bar, an imported craft product on the menu is a curation statement — it tells customers something about the venue. Premium Australian product fits that role like it was made for it.

4. The product needs no translation

Nobody needs English to enjoy a cold ginger beer on a Tokyo summer evening. The drink makes the first impression; the brand story can follow.

5. JAEPA does the maths for you

The Japan–Australia Economic Partnership Agreement gives Australian alcohol preferential import duty treatment. That's not marketing spin — it's margin, and it lets Australian brands price competitively against imports from countries without a free trade agreement.

The trade lanes are open, the affinity is decades old, and the appetite keeps growing. Japan isn't a moonshot for Australian craft. It's the next logical stop.
