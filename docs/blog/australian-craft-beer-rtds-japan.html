Five Reasons Australian Craft Beer and RTDs Are Built for the Japanese Market

Japanese Alcohol Market18 June 2026Aussie Bridge Japan5 min read

Not every export market suits every product. Japan and Australian craft beverages, however, are a natural match — for reasons that go deeper than taste.

1. Australian craft tells a place-based story

Japanese consumers have an unusually strong appreciation for regional provenance. The concept of terroir — that a product's character is shaped by the specific place it comes from — resonates deeply in Japan, where regional identity is celebrated across food, drink, and culture. Australian craft beer and RTDs, especially from distinctive regional producers, carry this naturally. The Kimberley. The Sunshine Coast. The high country. These places mean something.

2. The flavour profiles work

Japanese palates have evolved significantly over the past two decades. The growth of craft beer culture in Japan has created a consumer base that appreciates hop-forward flavours, fruit-driven profiles, and complexity. Australian craft beer — particularly tropical and citrus-forward styles — fits naturally into what Japanese craft drinkers are already seeking. Alcoholic ginger beer is a largely untapped category in Japan, which creates genuine white space for early movers.

3. Premium imported products carry status

In Japan's on-premise market, having an imported craft product on your menu is a statement. It signals a level of curation and quality that matters to hospitality operators and their customers. Australian craft, with its clean production standards and distinctive character, positions well as a premium import.

4. No language barrier for the product itself

Beer and RTDs do not require translation to be enjoyed. A Japanese consumer does not need to speak English to appreciate a well-made ginger beer on a hot day. The sensory experience is the entry point — the brand story can follow.

5. JAEPA means competitive pricing

The Japan-Australia Economic Partnership Agreement provides preferential import duty treatment for Australian alcohol entering Japan. This commercial advantage means Australian products can be priced competitively relative to imports from countries without a free trade agreement — a real and tangible benefit for brands entering the market now.

Australia and Japan have been trading partners for decades. The cultural affinity is established. The commercial framework is in place. The consumer appetite is growing. For Australian craft beverage brands, Japan is not a distant ambition — it is a realistic next step.
