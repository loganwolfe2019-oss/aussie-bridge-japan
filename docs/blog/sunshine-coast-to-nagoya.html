Sunshine Coast to Nagoya — How We Get Australian Flavour to Japanese Tables

Beverage Distribution15 June 2026Founder, Aussie Bridge Japan4 min read

The question I get most from brands: how does a can filled in Queensland actually end up on a bar counter in Nagoya? Here's the honest, unglamorous answer.

Queensland — where the paperwork wins or loses it

We're based on the Sunshine Coast, and everything on the Australian side runs through us — coordinating the producer, engaging a freight forwarder who actually knows alcohol exports, and getting every document right before anything leaves the country.

And the documents matter more than most brands realise. Export Declaration for the Australian Border Force. Certificate of Origin to claim JAEPA duty rates. A correctly structured commercial invoice and packing list. Each one has a job to do, and one wrong document can park your shipment at Japanese customs for weeks. So we don't get them wrong.

The crossing

Sea freight runs roughly 10 to 18 days depending on route and port. Alcohol is specialised cargo — temperature, container spec, insurance — so we only work with forwarders who treat it that way.

Nagoya — where our partner takes over

Once the ship docks, our family-owned Japanese distribution partner handles everything: customs clearance, liquor tax, food import notifications, port to warehouse, and onward distribution to trade customers through their wholesale network.

We chose Nagoya deliberately. It sits between Tokyo and Osaka with clean reach in every direction — north, south, east, west — without big-metro cost and complexity.

The last metre

From the Nagoya warehouse, product flows to bars, restaurants, hotels, and retailers. As your brand builds demand, the footprint grows with it.

None of this is simple. That's rather the point. The supply chain is the product we offer — and owning it end to end is what lets the brands we represent focus on making something worth shipping.
