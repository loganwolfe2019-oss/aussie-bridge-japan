Sunshine Coast to Nagoya — How We Get Australian Flavour to Japanese Tables

Beverage Distribution15 June 2026Aussie Bridge Japan5 min read

One of the most common questions we get from Australian brands considering Japan is: how does it actually work? How does a product made in Queensland end up on a bar counter in Nagoya?

The honest answer is that it takes a functioning supply chain, trusted relationships at both ends, and meticulous attention to documentation. Here is how Aussie Bridge Japan manages that journey.

It starts with us, in Queensland

Aussie Bridge Japan is based on the Sunshine Coast, Queensland. When a brand joins our portfolio, we manage everything on the Australian side — coordinating with the producer, engaging a freight forwarder experienced in alcohol exports, and ensuring every document is correct before anything leaves Australian soil.

The paperwork matters more than most brands realise. An Export Declaration with the Australian Border Force, a Certificate of Origin to claim preferential import duty rates under the Japan-Australia Economic Partnership Agreement, a correctly structured commercial invoice and packing list — every document has a job to do, and a missing or incorrect document can delay a shipment at Japanese customs by weeks.

Across the Pacific

Sea freight from Queensland to Japan takes approximately 10 to 18 days depending on the route and port. We use experienced freight forwarders who understand alcohol as a specialised cargo — temperature management, correct container specification, and insurance are all built into how we move product.

Into Japan through Nagoya

When the shipment arrives in Japan, our family-owned Japanese distribution partner takes over entirely. Headquartered in Nagoya, they manage everything from that point — customs clearance, liquor tax payment, food import notifications, transport from port to warehouse, and ultimately distribution to trade customers through their established partner network.

Nagoya is our hub by design. Centrally located between Tokyo and Osaka, it gives our partner's distribution network logical reach across Japan — north, south, east, and west — without the cost and complexity of operating from a major metropolitan centre.

To the customer

From our partner's Nagoya warehouse, product moves to bars, restaurants, hotels, retailers, and hospitality groups through their wholesale network. As each brand builds its reputation and demand in Japan, that distribution footprint expands.

The supply chain from Sunshine Coast to Nagoya is not simple. But it is ours — and that end-to-end ownership of the process is one of the most important things we offer the brands we represent.
