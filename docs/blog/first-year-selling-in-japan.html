What to Expect in Your First Year Selling in Japan — A Realistic Guide for Australian Brands

Market Entry Strategy22 June 2026Aussie Bridge Japan5 min read

Japan rewards patience. That is the single most important thing any Australian brand needs to understand before entering this market.

This is not a reflection on the quality of Australian products — it is a reflection of how Japanese business culture works. Relationships are built carefully. Trust is established through consistent follow-through. A bar owner who tries your product in month three might place their first regular order in month nine. That is not failure — that is how Japan works, and the brands that understand this from the start are the ones that build something lasting.

Here is what a realistic first year in Japan looks like for a brand working with Aussie Bridge Japan.

Months 1 to 3 — Getting the foundations right

Before a single bottle reaches Japan, there is groundwork to lay. Japanese label compliance. Food import notifications. Freight and logistics coordination. Product positioning and pricing for the Japanese market. This phase is less visible but entirely necessary — skipping or rushing it creates problems that are expensive to fix later.

Months 3 to 6 — First introductions and tastings

Through our partner network, your product begins reaching buyers. Tastings with bar owners, restaurant managers, and hospitality buyers. First conversations. Honest feedback about what resonates and what needs adjustment. This phase is about learning as much as selling.

Months 6 to 9 — First regular accounts

The buyers who responded well to the tasting begin placing first orders. These are small — Japan's hospitality market operates on frequent small orders, not large quarterly purchases. This is by design: operators manage tight storage, so they order little and often. Your supply chain needs to be built for this.

Months 9 to 12 — Building momentum

With a handful of active accounts reordering, the brand begins to develop a presence. Word of mouth in Japan's hospitality community moves slowly but steadily. Accounts recommend you to other accounts. Our partner network opens doors that cold outreach cannot.

What success looks like at the end of year one

Not a full container selling every month. More likely: 8 to 15 active accounts, a clear understanding of which product in your range performs best in Japan, a pricing structure that works, and a supply chain that operates smoothly. That is a foundation — and in Japan, a solid foundation is worth more than a fast start that burns out.

Aussie Bridge Japan exists to help Australian brands build that foundation correctly from day one. Japan is a long game. We are here to play it with you.
