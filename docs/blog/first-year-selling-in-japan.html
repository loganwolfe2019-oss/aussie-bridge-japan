What to Expect in Your First Year Selling in Japan — A Realistic Guide for Australian Brands

Market Entry Strategy22 June 2026Founder, Aussie Bridge Japan4 min read

I'll be straight with you, because I'd rather lose you on this page than in month six: Japan is slow. Deliberately, wonderfully slow.

A bar owner who tastes your beer in March might place their first order in November. In Australia that feels like a dead lead. In Japan it means they're taking you seriously — they're watching to see whether you'll still be around when they're ready. The brands that win here are the ones who know that walking in.

So here's what a realistic first year actually looks like.

Months 1–3: paperwork, not parties

Label compliance. Food import notifications. Freight coordination. Pricing for the Japanese market. None of it is glamorous, and all of it decides whether month nine goes well. Rush this phase and you'll pay for it twice later.

Months 3–6: tastings and honest feedback

Your product starts landing in front of real buyers through our partner network — bar owners, restaurant managers, hospitality groups. Some of the feedback will sting. Listen anyway. This phase is for learning as much as selling.

Months 6–9: first real accounts

The orders start, and they'll be smaller than you expect. Japanese venues order little and often — storage is tight and consistency matters more than volume. Build your supply chain for that rhythm, not for quarterly pallets.

Months 9–12: the flywheel starts

A handful of accounts reorder. Then they talk. Word of mouth in Japanese hospitality moves quietly, but it moves — and one operator's recommendation to another opens doors no cold email ever will.

What a good year one actually looks like

Eight to fifteen accounts that keep reordering. A clear read on which product in your range Japan actually wants. Pricing that holds. A supply chain that doesn't wobble. That's not a slow start — in Japan, that's a foundation.

Foundations are what we build. Japan is a long game, and we're here to play it with you.
